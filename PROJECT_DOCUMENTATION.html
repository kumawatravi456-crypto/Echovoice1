1. Abstract

Individuals suffering from speech and motor disabilities—such as Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS), cerebral palsy, Parkinson's disease, or stroke-induced dysarthria—often face immense barriers communicating with traditional voice-recognition assistants. Project Echo introduces an end-to-end multimodal conversation assistant that bridges non-verbal intent and live spoken dialogue.

By synchronously capturing (1) real-time speech via Web Speech recognition, (2) on-device hand gesture tracking via Google MediaPipe, and (3) facial mouth-motion metrics, the system derives user intent locally. A secure FastAPI backend feeds these signals into a conversational Large Language Model (LLM) to suggest context-aware responses. Crucially, a human-in-the-loop review safeguard guarantees that no artificial message is vocalized until explicitly confirmed by the user.

2. Problem Statement & Need

  • Failure of Standard ASR: Mainstream speech recognition systems rely on acoustic models trained exclusively on neurotypical voices and completely misinterpret atypical speech.
  • Sluggish Traditional AAC Devices: Conventional Augmentative and Alternative Communication devices require tedious letter-by-letter or symbol-by-symbol selection, causing average communication rates below 10 words per minute.
  • Privacy Concerns: Transmitting continuous ambient webcam video to remote cloud servers creates severe privacy and ethical issues for users and their families.

3. System Architecture

The architecture follows an edge-first pattern to preserve bandwidth and privacy:

  1. Client Vision Tracking: Runs `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` directly in the browser via Web Workers to identify 21 hand landmarks and 468 facial points without streaming raw video.
  2. Feature Compression: Calculates a 7-dimensional geometry vector (inter-finger Euclidean distances and palm orientations).
  3. Backend REST Inference: FastAPI consumes the 7-feature payload and evaluates it using a pre-trained scikit-learn classifier in under 10 milliseconds.
  4. Contextual AI Formulation: Groq LLM evaluates dialogue history and the latest verified gesture to draft concise responses.
  5. Confirmation & Audio Output: Upon explicit user tap, the confirmed phrase is vocalized via browser speech synthesis or ElevenLabs audio output.

5. Gesture Recognition Classes & Logic

The custom machine learning classifier targets five essential functional gestures:

Gesture Label Physical Expression System Interpretation
YES Closed fist with upright thumb extended upward Affirmative agreement / approval
NO Index finger raised and waved side-to-side Decline / negative response
HELP Flat open palm held vertically in front of body Urgent assistance requested
WATER Three central fingers raised in 'W' formation Hydration / physical comfort request
STOP Open palm pushed outward toward camera Cease current activity or conversation

7. Conclusion & Future Scope

Conclusion: Project Echo proves that fusing browser-level computer vision with lightweight machine learning pipelines creates a practical, privacy-respecting communication bridge for people with speech impairments. The application achieves responsive real-time inference without cloud video streaming.

Future Scope:

  • Expanding gesture vocabulary to full Indian Sign Language (ISL) alphabets and numbers.
  • Integrating offline WebAssembly ONNX runtimes for completely serverless local inference.
  • Refining visual lip-reading models to support silent speech decoding.
